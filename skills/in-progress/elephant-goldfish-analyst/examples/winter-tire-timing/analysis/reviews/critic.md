# Critic review (drafts for all four stages, before revision)

- D1 (7 C date SD >= 7 days): tests weather dates, while staffing follows sales timing. Revised: the year-to-year SD of the October share itself is at least 2 points in at least 6 of 10 provinces.
- D2 (spring dates vary more than fall): near-trivial and distorted by dates piling up at the window start in mild provinces. Dropped.
- G1 (colder October, larger October share): October's mean is only known on Oct 31, after October staffing is set, so the decision cannot be in-season; no detrending; 2020 outlier; no per-province consistency. Revised: detrended within-province association, same sign in at least 6 provinces, holds without 2020; decision reworded for post-season review.
- G2 (the 7 C rule): it would just repeat G1, since the crossing date tracks October temperature. Revised: does the 7 C crossing add anything beyond October temperature (partial correlation)? Expected to fail.
- G3 (warm March, larger April share): same gaps as G1. Revised the same way.
- F1 (March temperature predicts the April split): pooled vs per-province slope unclear; no absolute bar; 2020; April 1 may be later than real commit dates. Revised: pooled slope with province intercepts, expanding window of earlier years only, at least 10% and 0.2 points better than the province average, with and without 2020. The April 1 timing caveat stays in the paper.
- F2 (September predicts October): expected to fail; kept as a test of a tempting rule.
- P1 (allocation by F1): symmetric share error duplicates F1; no cost. Revised: asymmetric cost (under-planning April costs 2x over-planning), at least 5% lower total cost (set from exploration, where the cut was 7%) and wins in 60% of province-years, with and without 2020.
- Cross-cutting: 8 years per province; one city's weather per province; NAICS 4413 includes parts and accessories stores; industry sales are not the user's own stores.
