# Critic goldfish review, batch 1 (claims as first drafted, before revision)

Summary of the critic's reply per claim, and the revision each one caused.

- D1: near-trivial (a flat year gives Oct+Nov 16.7%, so 18% passes almost by construction); the decision needs fall to beat spring. Revised: Oct+Nov >= 18% AND above Apr+May in every province.
- D2: majority is not the quantity a budget decision uses; the share's size and trend are. Revised: Prairie majority in every year (D2) plus a separate trend claim (D4).
- D3: largely restates known weather; no effect size. Revised: adds a 2 pp minimum mean gap.
- G1: trivial given D2, because a majority region gets most of the dollar change at equal growth rates; two endpoints only. Revised: Prairie growth rate beats Atlantic's across every endpoint pair at least 3 years apart.
- G2: rho >= 0.3 is near-guaranteed by shared inflation and shocks, and too weak for dropping regional forecasts. Revised: rho >= 0.5 with 2020-2021 excluded (risky; explore rho 0.39).
- F1: no margin over naive; ambiguity on information at forecast origin. Revised: drift must beat naive by >= 10% relative MAPE; forecasts use only data before the target month (2020 kept in, noted).
- F2: pooled province-years let the Atlantic carry the Prairies; October's extra day and earlier Prairie snow favour October. Revised: Atlantic provinces only, each province separately >= 75%.
- F3: 1.5 pp absolute error is trivial for small provinces; no baseline. Revised: fall-share predictor must beat the annual-share predictor on mean relative error.
- R1: no status-quo comparison, no costs, sales are censored demand. Revised: drift+5% vs status-quo naive+5%, cost = overstock + 3 x shortfall (stated assumption), six-province total only.
- R2: no effect-size floor. Revised: fall-share allocation must cut misallocation by >= 20% and win >= 70% of years.
