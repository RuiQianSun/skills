# What practitioners already know (US light-vehicle fuel economy)

- Bigger engines (displacement, cylinders) use more fuel; hybrids use much less.
- Heavier, taller classes (pickups, large SUVs, vans) get lower MPG than cars.
- All-wheel and four-wheel drive cost some MPG versus two-wheel drive.
- Turbocharged small engines replaced larger naturally aspirated ones through the 2010s ("downsizing").
- Fleet MPG has crept up over time under CAFE standards.
- Rows are configurations, not sales: a niche sports car counts as much as a best-seller.
